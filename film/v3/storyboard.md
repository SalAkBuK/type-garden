# ROSE GARDEN — V3: TENDER VIOLENCE

**A 40-second living inscription: seduction, wound, rupture, absence, return.**

Rose Garden's most distinctive idea is that a letter can be an organism. A vine grips its stroke. A pause becomes a flower. A visitor leaves liquid on its surface. Its beauty has an edge, and its structure can survive the loss of that beauty. I want the film to make that feel inevitable before the audience even knows they are looking at typography.

The name **TENDER VIOLENCE** is the internal creative concept, not an on-screen tagline. The finished film carries only the planted **ROSE GARDEN** identity and the required attribution.

## Why this direction

The strongest proprietary image is the vine physically weaving around scanned letter ink. The strongest underused movement is **Gust**: a native wind front bends the canes and strips spinning petals across the picture. The strongest emotional reversal is **Wither**: the gorgeous garden becomes an ash-coloured thorn structure, then grows back from the same seeded architecture.

These give the edit its own geometry and dramatic stakes. The film begins close enough for a serif to feel like white architecture; it travels through petal and wing geometry, lets a real wound interrupt its composure, and breaks that composure with wind. A hard cut to bone makes the same garden severe. The 2.5-second bare-cane hold creates room for the return to feel excessive. Only then do the fragments cohere into a brand.

V1 already explored many materials, looks and mechanisms. V2 found intimacy in one butterfly, one drop and one lasting stain. V3 takes that intimate wound as one brief movement inside a larger choreography. Its central subject is the rose-letter organism, and its signature is the shift from seductive detail to violent directional movement to absolute composure.

## Film grammar

- **Canvas:** 16:9. Proposed final master 3840 × 2160, 60 fps. Exactly 40.000 seconds / 2,400 frames. The present proof images are 960 × 540.
- **Clock:** 96 BPM, 16 bars / 64 beats. Major scene cuts land on integer final frames. The four closing accents are editorial subdivisions and will be snapped to the final frame grid.
- **Colour:** Crimson gothic → Mourning → Crimson gothic. Black, white, deep crimson, living green, warm bone and dead umber. The bone passage is a structural negative, not a preset parade.
- **Type:** Actual Playfair Display Bold letter geometry. Giant fragments act as sets and trellises. No synthetic glyph morph, captions, feature titles or settings controls.
- **Camera:** Short tracks, diagonal crops, pushes and retreats, controlled composition roll, one wind-driven whip, then a stopped final camera. All are real transforms over the renderer's illustrated scene. No invented 3D orbit.
- **Editing:** Match curves, tangents and silhouettes. Cuts preserve direction or deliberately reverse it. Avoid generic easing on every shot; the final motion draft will distinguish searching, accelerating, braking and held movement.
- **Sound:** An original tactile score sketch, no narration. Close friction and wingbeats lead into low pulse, a near-silent wound, granular liquid rhythm, torn air, brittle dry decay, a genuine quiet interval, then a widening harmonic return. No keyboard-click demonstration.

## Three signature moments

**The architectural coil.** A clean white surface and a vine casting a shadow become an impossible landscape. The viewer discovers the letter through the winding plant, rather than through a typed title.

**The bone cut.** A petal and rose silhouette keep their screen position as the world goes from crimson darkness to black velvet on bone. This is an authored editorial discontinuity using the same genuine seeded composition. It does not suggest that wind or a bite automatically changes the application's look.

**The missing garden.** The thorn is almost all that remains for a full quiet interval. The return is not decorative filler: the native regrowth phase becomes the release after a real loss of screen energy. Five seconds of final composure is earned by the previous acceleration.

## What is real and what is film direction

Every proof flower, petal, vine, leaf, thorn, glyph, butterfly, drop and stain is drawn by the existing standalone renderer. Its material is Ink and its liquid behavior is Fluid. Research covered all four materials and seven looks, but this film does not need to display them all.

The film adds framing, composition rotation, time remapping, cuts and an original synthetic sound sketch. Native motions are selected and edited into this arc; the app does not trigger wind or death as a consequence of the butterfly. Rose Garden has projected petal depth inside an illustrated Canvas scene, not an orbitable 3D landscape. No external generated flower imagery or stock transition graphics have been substituted.

The existing application and both previous films are preserved. V3 reuses the existing deterministic capture and encounter scouting code read-only, and writes exclusively under `film/v3`.
