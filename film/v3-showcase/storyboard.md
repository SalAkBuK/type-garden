# ROSE GARDEN — V3 showcase direction

**SAME WORDS, MANY WORLDS**

A 40-second product showcase filmed as a living work of graphic design. We first make the promise unmistakable: someone opens the real application, types **in bloom**, and the letters become a trellis for a growing garden. Once that relationship is understood, the same phrase becomes the stable subject of an increasingly ambitious transformation sequence. The name above is an internal direction, not an on-screen slogan.

The strongest film is the one in which the audience can admire the images and still remember what caused them. The first six seconds give us that cause. The butterfly passage gives the garden a second, stranger kind of agency. Repeated framing then makes the extraordinary range understandable without a tour of controls. Each change has a visible reference point: the same words, the same seeded writing record, and a familiar place to look.

This is a separate V3 direction under `film/v3-showcase`. The existing **TENDER VIOLENCE** research, storyboard, review page, stills, tools and animatic remain under `film/v3`; V1, V2 and the application are preserved. This package is a storyboard/contact-sheet review. No continuous film or final-resolution frame sequence is authorized by this pass.

## Film language

- **Format:** 16:9; proposed master 3840 × 2160 at 60 fps; exactly 40 seconds / 2,400 frames. Draft stills use the real renderer at a smaller review size.
- **Primary subject:** lowercase **in bloom**, seed 7, retained through the growth, beauty, encounter, world changes, material passages and botanical crescendo. Only the final identity changes to planted **ROSE GARDEN**.
- **Opening:** a brief view of the actual application, its real empty writing surface and real text entry. The existing application branding is preserved. The camera then commits to the artwork. No fabricated dashboard, browser launch animation or renamed controls.
- **Visual refrain:** a readable wide portrait with the phrase in the same part of the frame. Macro shots leave it; match cuts bring us back. Hold scale and camera during the Look cuts. During font cuts, retain the baseline and phrase center while the actual new glyph widths and botanical fitting visibly change.
- **Camera:** measured translations, scales, crops and restrained image-plane rotation over the native Canvas scene. Short tracks have distinct acceleration and braking; the encounter camera waits for physical contact. The renderer does not offer a free 3D orbit.
- **Transitions:** real rose/petal occlusion, a vine crossing a stroke, and native liquid contours motivate film cuts or compositing masks. These are editorial transitions; a bite does not automatically change a Look, material or font in the application.
- **Typography:** planted native glyphs are the principal typography. No feature labels, names of presets, step numbers or settings-panel tutorial. Storyboard captions and material comparisons belong to the review package only.
- **Sound proposal:** intimate keystrokes establish authorship; plant friction, a widening tonal bed and an understated pulse carry growth. Wingbeats interrupt the pulse. Contact, release and impact are separately voiced. The impact becomes the first full rhythmic accent; the world montage adds speed, the materials briefly bring the listener close again, and the botanical crescendo resolves into a warm, sustained final chord. No voiceover. Sound is a proposed structure, not a finished mix.

## Revision note — butterfly chain and botanical climax

After the first contact sheet, stills 13–17 and 45–46 were recaptured through the same native renderer (no application or renderer change):

- **13–17:** at the original framing the butterfly was a speck and the landing, bite and bead looked nearly identical. They now step from a glide above the rose, to a rose-scale landing, to a tighter contact, to a macro of the real glossy bead, to the real drop falling. The bead is small in the native engine; the camera moves toward it and nothing is drawn or enlarged.
- **45:** the maximum-slider preset buried the phrase. The film's full-bloom state uses roses 1.7, size 1.05–1.40 and clusters 1.6 instead, which stays lush with the words still readable.
- **46:** now a vine-and-thorn macro over the actual letter. The Gust petals that were in this still remain in the plan as the transition out of the botanical passage, not in the still.

## Material proof and continuous-motion checkpoint

Each material needs **at least two proof images**, early contact and evolved response, at matched camera and glyph placement. A beautiful endpoint alone does not prove absorption, wicking or beading. The contact sheet should place each pair together. Captures must use separate fresh native simulations with the desired material selected before the event: changing a material after the previous soak can preserve or dry existing liquid and is not an equivalent comparison.

The four film passages are experiments on the same garden, edited together. Every early/evolved pair must share that experiment's text, seed, font, rose target, material, bleeding style, liquid color and camera. Only physical native simulation time advances. Blotting and Linen retain crimson so their material difference is easy to compare. Silver ichor and Fluid receive their own clear Ink passage. Wax retains crimson for a readable bead silhouette. Record the exact native sample times, target and collision positions in capture metadata.

The 48-still review capture uses the same supported **Fluid-based custom effect with Amount = 3** for all four material experiments. That control is native to the application and changes how much the rose bleeds; no gravity, bead pinning, collision or material physics has been altered. At the default amount, this Wax encounter formed pinned beads without a clear runoff. The supported higher amount supplied enough liquid for the existing bead engine to produce an actual detached runoff drop, visible in proof 42 with a null source-rose ID. The earlier full butterfly encounter remains default Legacy crimson, so its straight surface trace and the later Fluid-based silver-ichor Ink passage are distinct native bleeding behaviors.

After this storyboard is reviewed, the next pass is a low-resolution continuous motion draft: prove the opening's legible chronology, the entire butterfly causal chain, real material evolution, clean font loading/refitting, petal and vine transitions, and the final title/credit reading time. There is no expensive full render in this package. A continuous draft still requires review before final-resolution production.
